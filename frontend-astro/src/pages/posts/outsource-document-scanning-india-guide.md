Organizations usually outsource document scanning when the archive is too large, too urgent or too operationally distracting to handle internally.

Buying scanners and hiring temporary staff may look simple, but the real work includes preparation, batching, page sequence checks, OCR, metadata capture, file naming, quality control and secure delivery.

## When outsourcing makes sense

Outsource document scanning when you have:

- Record rooms or cartons waiting for conversion
- Multi-branch archives
- KYC or loan-file backlogs
- MRD or hospital records
- Legal or finance records
- A fixed migration deadline
- No internal scanning team
- DMS, ERP, EMR or cloud import requirements

## Onsite or offsite outsourcing

Onsite scanning is better when records are confidential, active, restricted or difficult to move. Offsite scanning can work when documents can be packed, transported and tracked securely.

The right model depends on sensitivity, page volume, city, available workspace, retrieval needs and timeline.

## Cost factors

Outsourced document scanning cost depends on:

- Page volume
- Paper condition
- Staples, tags, binding and preparation
- Black-and-white or color scanning
- OCR and searchable PDF
- Metadata fields
- Manual verification
- Onsite setup
- Timeline and daily output target
