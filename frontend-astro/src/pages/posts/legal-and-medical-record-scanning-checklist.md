Legal and medical records need more care than ordinary office files. These records are confidential, often active, and sometimes required for urgent retrieval.

Before starting a scanning project, agree on handling rules, indexing fields, quality checks and output formats.

## Legal document scanning checklist

For legal records, confirm:

- Matter ID or case number
- Client or party name
- Court or department
- Contract type
- Document date
- Confidentiality rules
- Page sequence and annexure grouping
- Searchable PDF or PDF/A output
- DMS or legal repository import format

See [legal document scanning services](/legal-document-scanning-services) for a deeper service page.

## Medical records scanning checklist

For medical records, confirm:

- MRD number
- Patient ID
- Admission and discharge dates
- Department and doctor
- OPD/IPD file type
- Consent forms and reports
- Onsite scanning need
- EMR/HMS output format
- Retrieval support during scanning

See [medical records scanning services](/medical-records-scanning-services) and [medical record digitization services](/medical-record-digitization-services).

## Shared controls

Both legal and medical scanning should include:

- Restricted access
- Batch logs
- File movement tracking
- OCR and indexing rules
- Exception reporting
- Secure digital handover

## Final thought

Sensitive document scanning is successful when the process protects records and makes retrieval easier. Plan the index before scanning starts.
